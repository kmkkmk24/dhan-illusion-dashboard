Guide

How to read the Sectors tab

What this tab tells you

Which Nifty sectors are gaining strength compared with the overall market, which are losing it, and how that is changing. Every measure compares a sector index with Nifty 50, so "strong" always means "stronger than the market".

1 · Two measurements

RS-RatioIs the sector beating Nifty 50? Above 100 = yes, below = no. Further from 100 = by more.
RS-MomentumIs that getting better or worse? Above 100 = relative strength improving, below = fading.

2 · Four quadrants and the cycle

Improvingbehind · catching up → Leadingahead · gaining ↑ Nifty 50
= 100 ↓ Laggingbehind · falling back ← Weakeningahead · fading

Sectors usually travel clockwise: Improving → Leading → Weakening → Lagging → Improving. That is the "rotation". It is a tendency, not a rule: a sector can turn back, e.g. from Improving straight back to Lagging.

3 · The panels

Quadrant tiles
How many sectors sit in each quadrant right now, and which ones. Many sectors in Improving means strength is broadening; leaders sliding into Weakening is the first warning that leadership is changing.
Confirmed on Daily & Weekly
Sectors in the same quadrant on both timeframes: the Daily-vs-Weekly cross-check done for you. Sectors where the two disagree are listed under mixed readings.
Rotation map
The same quadrants as a picture. Each dot is a sector's latest position; its tail shows where it came from. Look at where a dot is (which quadrant, how far from the centre) and where it is heading (direction of the tail).
Money flow
The biggest movers on the latest bar: sectors rotating in (relative strength rising) and out (falling), including quadrant changes such as "Lagging → Improving". This is where new rotations show up first.
Performance heatmap
Plain returns over 1 day, 1 week, 1 month and 3 months. Use it to check the absolute move behind a relative reading.
Momentum leaderboard
A 0–100 score per sector (50% relative strength across hourly/daily/weekly/monthly + 50% price action such as EMAs and higher highs). The score says how strong a sector is now; the map says whether that is improving or fading.
Detail panel
Click any sector anywhere to see its score, recent returns, weekly chart and the stocks in it, including any active scanner signals.

4 · A simple way to use it

  1. Start with what's confirmed. The Confirmed on Daily & Weekly panel lists sectors whose short- and longer-term readings agree. Leading or Improving on both are the firmest strength readings.
  2. See where they're heading. On the map, switch between Weekly (bigger trend) and Daily (timing) to see each sector's direction. Treat mixed readings with more caution.
  3. What's changing: Money flow. Note sectors rotating in, and any quadrant changes on the latest bar.
  4. Reality check: Heatmap. Confirm the actual returns. Relative strength in a falling market can still mean falling prices.
  5. Drill down: Leaderboard and detail panel. Open the sectors you shortlisted, check the score and chart, and look at their stocks and signals.

5 · Keep in mind

  • Relative, not absolute. "Leading" means outperforming Nifty, not necessarily rising.
  • Near the lines is noisy. A dot close to 100 on either axis can switch quadrant on a small move; check the map, not just the tile.
  • "Money flow" is relative strength. Index data has no volume, so it shows performance rotating between sectors, not traded money.
  • Readings describe the market; they are not trade signals. Use them to decide where to look, then judge individual stocks on their own setups.
  • Freshness. Data updates when you press Refresh (and on the scheduled daily sector report). The date under the title shows which session it covers.
